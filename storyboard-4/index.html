Thesis

One electron, born in raw combustion, travels the grid intact and arrives as an idea landing in a single mind. The whole film is that one move: energy under control, delivered. Everything in the cut should serve the arrival.

The one directive that changes everything

Right now the board reads as 30 frames at a flat 2 second metronome. A metronome is a slideshow. The film only becomes a film when the cutting is variable. Slow and hypnotic through the journey, a held breath at the threshold, a fast eruption at the moment of release, then a slow hold on the landing. Lock that curve and the piece comes alive. Leave it even and it stays a deck.

The single biggest miss · the Fire moment

The climax currently gets two even beats, 25 and 26, cut at the same length as a transmission tower in a field. The climax must hold the most cuts in the film, not the fewest. Fragment the release into four to six fast cuts: the diode junction, the bloom building from a point, the lens flaring out, the burst into the dark hall. Sub second, kinetic, stacked. This is the one place to add frames rather than cut them. Everything upstream is the patient hand pulling back. This is the loose.

The landing · protect the inversion

The entire film is built on a reversal. The same cold, procedural machinery that moves raw power across a continent does not end in force. It ends in a face catching light and an idea taking hold. The closer the mechanics stay to a real industrial process, cold, exact, POV as the electron itself, the harder the warm turn lands at frame 28. Keep it cold and procedural all the way to the turn. Hold the cyan as the only living color in a steel world. Then let the warmth break in all at once at the face. Do not pre warm it. The discipline of the first 27 frames is what makes the last 3 mean something.

Motion and export

The deliverable decides the tool: a 4608 × 1536 (3:1) LED-wall film, 60s, cut to beats.mp3 with the drop on Fire at 1:45, silent picture with the music and designed sound laid in the edit. Three properties of that work against Veo 3, which was the earlier assumption.

The call: Runway Gen-4 image-to-video as primary, Kling 2.x as the cheaper backup. Veo 3 only if generated sound is ever wanted, which here it is not.

Aspect · never re-crop

Do not feed a 16:9 model the 3:1 and let it crop. Letterbox each 3:1 frame into the model's canvas (black top and bottom), animate, then crop the center 3:1 band back out. The bars are inert carrier, the content band moves, zero content lost. Model-agnostic, so the tool choice stays about motion and silence, not aspect.

Motion doctrine · forward through space

The spine of the film is one unbroken forward move through space, from the plant to the mind. The camera is the electron, always travelling forward, into the frame, toward the destination. The 30 cuts are waypoints on that single trajectory, not separate vignettes, so the motion vector stays consistent across the whole piece: we are always pushing ahead, never drifting sideways, never pulling back. The cut sheet only modulates the speed of that forward travel, it never reverses it.

So every frame is real generative motion in three dimensions, particles and depth streaming past as we advance. No flat 2D push or zoom on a still, that look is dead and cheap on point-cloud frames and it breaks the sense of travelling through real space. Controlled energy always, never chaotic, never a camera that shakes or races. Each clip is silent, holds the palette, adds nothing new, and ends where the cut needs it.

Brief structure, short and motion-only since the frame carries the content: controlled camera move, one subtle thing that moves, scene stays locked (no new objects, no people, no text, palette unchanged), deliberate controlled energy, no shake, no fast moves, no morphing. Generate ~5s silent clips, trim each to its beat-locked duration, place on the grid. Assembles down the same path as the existing LED-wall films, minus the crop step, since these frames are already native 3:1.

Resolution flow · 4K is a video step, not a still step

The stills are already 4608 × 1536, wider than DCI and UHD 4K, so they feed the animator as-is. Do not upscale them first: the video model downsamples any input to its own working resolution, so a bigger still is thrown away. Resolution drops at animation and is recovered afterward.
